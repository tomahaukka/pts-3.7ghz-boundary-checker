Executive Summary

As Nordic industrial enterprises accelerate their transition from legacy Wi-Fi and wired Ethernet to local Private 5G Non-Public Networks (NPN), enterprise system integrators face a major engineering hurdle: transitioning from IP-layer architecture to high-precision Physical Layer (RF) propagation and regulatory compliance.

While modern 3GPP Standalone (SA) architectures simplify core deployment, radio frequency (RF) design in industrial environments remains heavily constrained by physical topology and regulatory coexistence rules enforced by Post- och telestyrelsen (PTS) in Sweden. This whitepaper analyzes the RF propagation characteristics of the 3.7 GHz (n77/n78) mid-band versus the 26 GHz (n257/n258) mmWave spectrum allocations, outlining an automated workflow for evaluating boundary interference limits in the SWEREF 99 TM coordinate system.

1. Regulatory Context: PTS Sweden Spectrum Allocations

PTS provides distinct licensing frameworks for local private 5G deployments in Sweden, each targeting different industrial use cases and propagation mechanics.

1.1 The 3.7–3.8 GHz Band (Mid-Band)

  • Band Class: 3GPP n77 / n78 (Up to 80 MHz local licenses)
  • Primary Use Case: Wide-area industrial yards, indoor logistics, AGV/AMR fleet automation, and mobile machine-to-machine (M2M) connectivity.
  • Coexistence Challenges: Strict Out-of-Band (OOB) and co-channel Power Flux Density (PFD) / Received Signal Received Power (RSRP) limits at property boundaries to protect adjacent operators.

1.2 The 24.25–25.25 GHz Band (mmWave)

  • Band Class: 3GPP n257 / n258 (Up to 400 MHz channel allocation)
  • Primary Use Case: Ultra-high throughput links (e.g., multi-camera 4K machine vision inspection, real-time telemetry, dense sensor arrays).
  • Coexistence Challenges: Near-line-of-sight (NLOS) blockage, extreme atmospheric and structural attenuation, requiring high-density small cell deployments.

2. RF Propagation & Attenuation Physics

2.1 Path Loss Comparison: Mid-Band vs. mmWave

In free space, path loss follows the Free Space Path Loss (FSPL) equation derived from Friis' transmission formula:

FSPL (dB) = 20 · log10(d) + 20 · log10(f) - 27.55

Comparing 3,750 MHz (Mid-band) and 26,000 MHz (mmWave) over a 100-meter Line-of-Sight (LOS) distance:

  • 3.75 GHz FSPL at 100m: 20·log10(100) + 20·log10(3750) - 27.55 ≈ 83.93 dB
  • 26.00 GHz FSPL at 100m: 20·log10(100) + 20·log10(26000) - 27.55 ≈ 100.75 dB
Key Insight: At 100 meters, a 26 GHz mmWave signal experiences an additional 16.8 dB of free-space path loss compared to 3.7 GHz, requiring significantly higher Equivalent Isotropically Radiated Power (EIRP) or massive MIMO beamforming gain to maintain link budget parity.

2.2 Structural & Material Penetration Loss

Industrial topographies present complex attenuation vectors. In indoor-to-outdoor scenarios, external wall construction dominates boundary propagation:

Material Type Attenuation @ 3.7 GHz (dB) Attenuation @ 26 GHz (dB)
Standard Double Glazing 2 – 4 dB 8 – 15 dB
Energy-Efficient (IR-coated) Glass 18 – 28 dB 30 – 45+ dB
Reinforced Concrete (20cm) 20 – 35 dB > 50 dB (Complete Block)
Profiled Metal Cladding / Sandwich Panels 25 – 40+ dB > 60 dB

3. Coexistence & Boundary Compliance Modeling

Under PTS coexistence rules, a private 5G network operator must ensure that signal levels at adjacent property lines do not exceed permissible threshold values that could degrade neighboring networks.

RSRP_boundary (dBm) = EIRP_tx (dBm) - FSPL(d) - Wall_Loss (dB)

4. Automated Compliance Verification

To streamline PTS boundary verification for enterprise system integrators, an open-source evaluation engine was developed in Python by Radiometric Analytics. The engine integrates directly into GIS pipelines using SWEREF 99 TM (EPSG:3006) coordinates.

from pts_checker import PTSBoundaryChecker

# Initialize engine for Swedish 3.7 GHz spectrum
checker = PTSBoundaryChecker(freq_mhz=3750.0)

# Evaluate a transmitter near an industrial site boundary (SWEREF 99 TM)
result = checker.evaluate_boundary_point(
    tx_x=150000.0, tx_y=6400000.0,
    boundary_x=150120.0, boundary_y=6400000.0,
    eirp_dbm=24.0,       # Indoor Small Cell EIRP
    wall_loss_db=12.0    # Concrete/Glass envelope attenuation
)

print(f"Distance to Boundary:    {result['distance_m']} m")
print(f"Calculated FSPL:         {result['fspl_db']} dB")
print(f"Predicted Boundary RSRP: {result['rsrp_dbm']} dBm")

5. Conclusion & Recommendations

  1. Spectrum Selection Strategy: Use 3.7 GHz as the primary connectivity layer for site-wide mobility and AGVs. Reserve 26 GHz strictly for localized, line-of-sight high-bandwidth cells.
  2. Deterministic Modeling: Relying on default free-space calculations without factoring in wall losses and SWEREF 99 TM boundary distances leads to regulatory non-compliance.
  3. Automated Tooling: Incorporate automated boundary verification engines directly into RF survey dossiers to accelerate PTS approval workflows.
